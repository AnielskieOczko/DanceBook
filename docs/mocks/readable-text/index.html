Readable text styles

Issue #301. The “Recommendation” card in the figures mock reads sharp and comfortable, and the owner asked whether that look should become the way the app shows text. Below are four proposals, each drawn on the same four samples: a Note body, a figure’s technical remarks, a choreography description and a comment thread with a reply. Each is shown at desktop width and at 375px. Use the switch to see Polish.

Light only, as the app is. A Material is called a “Note”. Sample content is made up.

All four directions reuse existing tokens (--primary, --primary-soft, --surface-container, --outline-variant, the radius ladder, Inter and Source Serif 4). The missing pieces are a small named text scale and a measure token; they are listed in each proposal and again in the recommendation.

Recommendation: use A for short advice and remarks, and C for long reading. Reasoning is at the end of the page.

Recommendation

Do not make one container the app-wide rule. The card’s strength is that it frames one piece of advice; applied to every block of text it becomes a stack of identical bordered boxes, which is exactly the busy look the owner has been removing elsewhere. Split by what the text is.

WhereUseReason
Recommendations, tips, assistant replies, empty states, figure “technical remarks”A, the full cardShort, self-contained, one lead-in each. The card is the right size for them.
Note body, choreography description, long figure descriptionC, prose only, no cardThese are read for minutes. C’s larger size and spacing do the work, and the page stays calm. The pull-quote and numbered list carry the structure the card’s border would have.
Comments and step commentsToday’s compact list, with C’s type scaleMany short items in a row; a box around each one is too heavy. Hairline separators and a 36px avatar keep the thread scannable.
Dense tables, tooltips, short labels, form fieldsNo changeThe scale below is too large for them.

Why not B or D: B is the most pleasant to read but the tallest, and the serif at 17/28 makes a Note page about 30% longer than C. D needs width for the rail and has nothing to offer on a phone, where the rail simply stacks under the text. Both are good for a future “read mode” and are cheap to add later because they use only the tokens listed below.

The case against: a larger reading size costs vertical space, and on pages that mix a Note with its figures table the longer page pushes the table down. Comments and step comments should keep today’s density; if everything were 16/26 the Training and Notes lists would feel heavy. Source Serif 4 is already loaded, so B and D add no download.
