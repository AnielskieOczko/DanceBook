Assistant history and full-window view

Two directions for issue #215: a history panel that lists every conversation with a three-dot menu (Rename, Delete), and an expanded full-window view that collapses back to the side panel. Both extend the widget that exists today (bar, 400px panel, phone sheet) and use the approved rounded form language. Everything below is clickable: open a menu, rename, delete, expand, collapse.

Rules both directions follow
A title is plain text until you choose Rename in the three-dot menu. Then it becomes a field with Save and Cancel; Enter saves, Escape cancels, 80 characters at most. Rows are open buttons, so a tap never edits by accident.
Wording
A saved Material is a Note everywhere. Deleting a conversation never deletes what was saved from it, and the confirmation says so.
Direction A, Ledger
History is a view inside the panel, reached from the clock icon that already exists. Delete is confirmed inside the row. Expanded, the history stays on screen as a left column beside a centred reading column.
Direction B, Switcher
History hangs off the conversation title as a floating list, like a document switcher. Delete asks in a modal. Expanded, a right column keeps the Notes cited and the drafts of this conversation in view.
What the mock changes in the product
Adds an expand control to the panel header, a conversation search field, date groups in the list, and a three-dot menu in place of the always-visible rename form and Delete link. Rename happens in place.
Not covered
Pagination of the list (the product loads all of them; 16 are shown) and renaming from inside an open thread. Both are listed under decisions at the end.

Direction A: Ledger

The panel keeps one job at a time. The clock icon swaps the thread for the list; the arrow swaps it back. Expanding adds no new place to learn: the same list simply stays visible next to the thread.

History panel at 400px

Phone widths

The phone sheet is already full screen, so there is nothing to expand. Rows keep a 44px menu target. Delete confirms inside the row so the thumb stays where it was.

Expanded full-window view, live

Press the expand icon in the panel header. In the full window, use the collapse icon, press Escape, or close the assistant. The same conversation stays open and the page behind it is untouched. Below 800px wide the expand control is hidden, as it would be in the app.

  1. ExpandThe panel grows from its right edge to cover the window in 240ms. The list appears as a 292px column on the left.
  2. WorkRename and delete behave exactly as in the panel. Opening another conversation replaces only the centre column.
  3. CollapseThe collapse icon or Escape returns to the 400px panel on the same conversation, and focus goes back to the expand icon.

Direction B: Switcher

The conversation title in the header is the way to other conversations, as in a document editor. The thread stays on screen behind a floating list, so you never lose your place. Expanded, the extra width goes to a column of what this conversation produced.

Phone widths

On a phone the floating list becomes a bottom sheet that rises over the thread. Delete is a modal at every width.

Expanded full-window view, live

Expand from the panel header. In the full window the title opens the list as a 340px drawer from the left edge over the thread. Collapse with the icon or Escape.

  1. ExpandThe panel grows to the full window. The thread takes the centre; a 340px column on the right lists the Notes the assistant linked and the drafts it prepared.
  2. SwitchThe title opens a drawer with the same list, menu, rename and delete modal. It closes when you pick a conversation or press Escape.
  3. CollapseBack to the 400px panel on the same conversation. The right column folds away; its items stay reachable in the thread as cards.

Recommendation and open decisions

Recommendation: Direction A
It reuses the control that exists (the clock icon), keeps one thing on screen at a time in the panel, and makes the full window a plain widening of the same layout. Direction B's right column is attractive, but it adds a second place where drafts live, next to the cards that already hold Save and Edit in form.
Decisions for you
1. Should a title also be renameable from inside an open thread, or only from the list? Both mocks say list only.
2. Should the list search match message text or titles only? The mock shows titles.
3. Inline delete confirmation (A) or a modal (B), whichever direction wins?
4. Is the icon plus Escape enough to collapse, or should a click on the page behind a docked panel also close it?
